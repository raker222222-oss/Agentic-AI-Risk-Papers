Open Research Ideas on Agentic AI Risk

Rakesh Rajan (Rakesh OSS)

Research on how advanced AI agents can fail through wrong state reconstruction, bad retrieval, distorted interpretation, broken sequencing, unstable epistemic structure, recursive reasoning, and human-directed misuse—without requiring independent machine will.

Zenodo DOIGitHub repository

Master framework

Language is a lossy projection of state; reconstructing state from language is underdetermined; probable reconstruction is not necessarily epistemically warranted reconstruction.

AI Reconstructs Reality From Language—and Can Reconstruct It Wrong

Research ideas

How AI Distorts Meaning!

AI-Mediated Interpretive Displacement: how AI interpretation can become a hidden causal participant in human communication.

AI Amplifies Its Own Mistakes to Truth

Recursive Amplification: how an initial mistake can alter later evidence and appear to confirm itself.

Right Facts, Wrong Order, Wrong Action

Sequence Integrity: why preserving chronology, precedence, procedural order, and revision history is a distinct safety requirement.

Proof? Just Ask AI to Define It!

Evidentiary Threshold Distortion: how excessive demand for explicit proof can cause dangerous underreaction despite convergent evidence.

The Agent Has the Right Goal and the Wrong World

Agentic State-Model Divergence: how an aligned agent can act competently on the wrong representation of reality.

AI Reconstructs Reality From Language—and Can Reconstruct It Wrong

The master architectural framework linking underdetermined language-to-state reconstruction to prior substitution, epistemic distortion, state divergence, and recursive agentic failure.

The Annotation Tax

Relational Topology Loss: how LLMs can remember propositions while losing the epistemic, conditional, temporal, attributional, scope, or pragmatic relations that determine their meaning—and the extra annotation humans must supply to prevent or repair that loss.

Core proposition

lossy linguistic representation → underdetermined state reconstruction → prior-driven selection → coherence preservation → epistemic relation distortion → wrong operative state → competent action → feedback
