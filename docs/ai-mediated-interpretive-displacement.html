AI-Mediated Interpretive Displacement

Rakesh Rajan · Working paper

All papersFull paper on GitHubDOI

Abstract

Generative AI is increasingly used not only to draft messages but to interpret them. A person receives a text, asks an AI what it means, and may then respond to the AI's interpretation rather than to the original message. When both parties do this, AI can become an invisible third participant in the relationship.

Recent work already establishes this broader setting. Levkovich and Alon (2026) describe generative AI as a "third voice" in couple relationships, while Tseng and Liang (2026) document how people use AI for dating, conflict, communication, and relationship decisions. This paper isolates a narrower mechanism not captured by those studies.

AI-Mediated Interpretive Displacement (AMID) occurs when an AI-generated interpretation becomes more behaviorally important than the original communication it purports to explain. The central concern is not simply hallucination: an interpretation can alter human beliefs and responses, thereby changing the relationship and the evidence available to later interpretation, even when the original inference was wrong.

The paper develops a sequence model of this process, distinguishes interpretation from evidence, identifies reciprocal AI-mediated escalation, examines context compression and anomaly failure, and proposes falsifiable experiments across models and prompting conditions.

Core mechanism

Human communication → AI interpretation → belief shift → behavioral change → new relational evidence.

This differs from ordinary AI relationship advice. The model can become causally important before it recommends any decision at all: a request to explain what another person's words "really mean" may be enough to change how the relationship proceeds.
