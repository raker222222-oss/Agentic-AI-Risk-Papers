# Comparative Findings: Ten Investigations, Component-Level Evidence

## Core tenet: capability growth can change the sophistication of failures

**A central the backward investigation hypothesis is that an improving model may retain, suppress, compensate for, or transform an earlier weakness while acquiring the ability to express that weakness in more sophisticated or consequential ways.** Better overall benchmarks do not imply that all underlying control problems have been reduced to zero. Nor does a later, more elaborate failure require an identical earlier incident: backward analysis seeks simpler manifestations of the relevant failure family.

**Longitudinal variation, not a complete ancestral reconstruction (Google):** The *Gemini 1.5 Technical Report* notes that improved text understanding and instruction following may help explain susceptibility to certain human-readable adversarial instructions, even as other jailbreak resistance improved. The *Gemini 2.5 Technical Report* reports that greater Gemini 2.0 capability enabled some more effective natural-language attacks despite gains against other methods; subsequent adversarial training improved Gemini 2.5 defenses, while the more capable 2.5 Pro remained less resilient than 2.5 Flash on the reported tests. These observations concern *attack susceptibility*, not direct proof of the shutdown failure's internal origins. They furnish a longitudinal illustration of capability-dependent changes in failure expression and defense effectiveness. Sources: [Gemini 1.5 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05530); [Gemini 2.5 Technical Report](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemini/gemini_v2_5_report.pdf).

**Mandatory case-analysis question:** Starting with the latest documented failure, what simpler constraint, authority, goal, or evidence-handling imperfection can reasonably be inferred backward in named predecessor models? What new capability or scaffold could have changed the later manifestation? What published evidence favors elimination, suppression, transformation, or compensation? Record counterexamples and improvements, and distinguish observation from inference. Do not reverse the argument by starting with an old defect and attributing modern incidents to it.


## Scope and component-level interpretation

The first ten investigations were selected from publicly documented incidents, principally the METR incident list, and developed by the proposer of *Unmasking Rogues*. They are an exploratory, non-representative research collection, not a randomly sampled estimate of the method's success rate. Aggregate counts would describe this collection and its authorial judgments, not the prevalence of inherited failure mechanisms in frontier models.

A retrospective comparison should classify **each component rather than assigning one grade per incident**, using descriptive labels: **direct predecessor**, **developmental research connection**, **independent functional predecessor**, or **no informative predecessor identified**. It should also record whether the component was fixed before the predecessor search or revised later, the cited basis for the connection, and an observation that could challenge it. Since these analyses were not prospectively preregistered, a component's original definition should be marked **not independently documented** where the record does not establish when it was first proposed; do not retrospectively claim that it was fixed in advance.

A component with no identified predecessor is a legitimate outcome. Independent historical analogy must not be used to fill a missing model-lineage connection. The inventory records the explanatory usefulness and limits of the investigated examples, not a frequency of successful ancestry reconstruction.

## Purpose

This chapter compares the backward investigation's application to four distinct incidents. It does not treat those incidents as interchangeable instances of a single defect and does not assert that the foundational theory has been confirmed.

## the backward investigation theory: persistence, suppression, transformation and compensation

the backward investigation is a broad, retrospective **theory under examination across multiple documented cases**, not a claim to have established internal causation in any particular incident. Each study begins with an **identified incident model** (version and, when available, checkpoint), a primary incident record, and a documented developmental or research lineage. Cases that identify only a product, agent or provider are deferred. Earlier manifestations need not reproduce the endpoint incident: the theory asks whether **simpler limitations in the same failure family** can be observed in earlier generations and whether added training, reasoning, constraints, tool access and scaffolding could alter their expression or consequences.

the backward investigation distinguishes four possibilities:

- **Elimination:** a weakness has genuinely ceased to operate.
- **Suppression:** its measured manifestations decline under tested conditions, but it may still occur elsewhere.
- **Transformation:** a related weakness changes form or consequences as capabilities and environments change.
- **Compensation:** another model component, external control or scaffold prevents a remaining weakness from producing an observable failure.

These are competing possibilities, **not assumptions that all earlier flaws persist**. Successive training, fine-tuning, alignment, reinforcement learning, instruction hierarchies and scaffolding may improve measured reliability without proving a primitive issue has been nullified to zero. Zero failures in finite testing mean zero **observed** failures in those conditions, not demonstrated universal elimination. The relevant question is whether interventions eliminate, suppress, transform or compensate for the weakness; public observations often cannot distinguish them.

The case studies test the **explanatory reach and limits of this theory collectively**. Public reports, model cards, appendices and independent evaluations can support or challenge a proposed developmental pattern; behavioral resemblance alone cannot prove training inheritance or internal causation. More complete technical validation by researchers with model access may involve intermediate checkpoints, training records, interventions and representation studies. A backward inquiry could ultimately extend beyond model versions into learning or language acquisition, but that remains an open hypothesis, not an established endpoint.

## Common methodological result

The cases demonstrate why the investigation must begin with the *specific recorded action* and then follow only mechanisms warranted by evidence. Shared verbal labels such as “misalignment,” “goal seeking,” or “reward hacking” do not identify common ancestry. A case may bifurcate into several explanations: model behavior, training and evaluation, orchestration, human authority assignment, or tool permissions.

## Two tracks, never a merged chronology

**Track 1: Primary trace.** Record model generation, checkpoint or training-stage evidence, observed behavior, safeguards, and any documented transmission between stages. Investigate multi-hop bridges without converting a citation or analogy into behavioral causation.

**Track 2: External comparative archive.** Record earlier similar exploits in symbolic AI, evolutionary systems, reinforcement learning, or software testing. These establish that comparable failure forms existed; they do not move a lineage's endpoint.

A date such as 1983 may be the earliest known *external parallel* and still have no bearing on where the *primary model trace* stops.

## Hypothesis testing and falsification

To test the latent-ancestral-failure hypothesis, researchers with relevant model access may reconstruct predecessor checkpoints wherever access allows, use comparable tasks with controlled permissions and evaluation conditions, record negative as well as positive results, and test rival explanations. Critical outcomes include identifying an inherited or reintroduced vulnerability, showing effective correction, or finding that an incident is entirely explained by system-level access failures.

## Cross-case findings and present status

The research programme is currently **exploratory**. It yields a consistent investigative standard, documented contemporary incidents, several plausible historical bridges, and specific experimental questions. It does not yet demonstrate a continuous causal path across each model series, nor that all four failures have a common root.

## Research agenda

1. Construct a case-specific, dated primary lineage ledger for each model family.
2. Separate model behavior from tool, grader, and access-control failures.
3. Reconstruct and test candidate primitive failures against predecessor checkpoints.
4. Record external analogues in independent evidence appendices.
5. Publish negative results and unresolved bridges alongside positive connections.
